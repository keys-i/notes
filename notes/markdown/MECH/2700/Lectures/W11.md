# Week 11 - Hyperbolic Equations

## Hyperbolic Behaviour

Hyperbolic PDEs describe waves and transport where information travels at a
finite speed

Discontinuities propagate rather than being immediately smoothed, and nonlinear
waves can steepen into shocks

Canonical examples are

$$
\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial t^2}
=c^2\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}
$$

and the one-way convection equation

$$
\boxed{\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}
+v\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}=0}
$$

For constant $v$, the exact solution is a translated initial profile

$$
u(x,t)=u_0(x-vt)
$$

The sign of $v$ determines the upwind direction and the boundary where
information enters

## First-Order Convection

Define the Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy number

$$
\boxed{C=\frac{v\Delta t}{\Delta x}}
$$

It compares the physical distance travelled in one time step with the grid spacing

### Upwind Scheme for $v>0$

Use a backward spatial difference because information arrives from the left

$$
\frac{u_i^{m+1}-u_i^m}{\Delta t}
+v\frac{u_i^m-u_{i-1}^m}{\Delta x}=0
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{u_i^{m+1}=(1-C)u_i^m+Cu_{i-1}^m}
$$

The scheme is stable for

$$
\boxed{0\le C\le1}
$$

For $v<0$, information comes from the right, so use a forward spatial difference

$$
u_i^{m+1}=(1+C)u_i^m-Cu_{i+1}^m,
\qquad -1\le C\le0
$$

Together, the upwind stability condition is

$$
\boxed{|C|\le1}
$$

### Why Upwind Direction Matters

The numerical domain of dependence must include the physical domain of dependence

Using a downwind difference samples information from the wrong side and is
unstable for the explicit convection scheme

At $C=1$, the positive-velocity upwind update becomes

$$
u_i^{m+1}=u_{i-1}^m
$$

so the profile moves exactly one grid cell per time step without numerical diffusion

### Numerical Diffusion

For $0<C<1$, the upwind method smears sharp gradients

Its modified equation contains an artificial diffusion term with coefficient

$$
D_{\text{num}}=\frac{v\Delta x}{2}(1-C)
$$

The method is stable when this artificial diffusion is non-negative

> [!NOTE] Stability and accuracy are different
>
> A stable upwind result can still be visibly over-diffused, especially on a
> coarse grid

## Second-Order Wave Equation

For

$$
u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}
$$

use central differences in both time and space

$$
\frac{u_i^{m+1}-2u_i^m+u_i^{m-1}}{\Delta t^2}
=c^2
\frac{u_{i+1}^m-2u_i^m+u_{i-1}^m}{\Delta x^2}
$$

Define

$$
C=\frac{c\Delta t}{\Delta x}
$$

and rearrange

$$
\boxed{
u_i^{m+1}
=2(1-C^2)u_i^m
+C^2(u_{i-1}^m+u_{i+1}^m)
-u_i^{m-1}}
$$

This scheme needs two previous time levels and is stable in one spatial
dimension when

$$
\boxed{C\le1}
$$

### Starting the Wave Scheme

Initial displacement supplies

$$
u_i^0=f(x_i)
$$

Initial velocity supplies

$$
u_t(x_i,0)=g(x_i)
$$

A second-order first step is

$$
\boxed{
u_i^1
=u_i^0+\Delta t\,g_i
+\frac{C^2}{2}(u_{i-1}^0-2u_i^0+u_{i+1}^0)}
$$

Using the general three-level update without constructing $u^1$ is a common
implementation error

### Two Spatial Dimensions

For

$$
u_{tt}=c^2(u_{xx}+u_{yy})
$$

the central update is

$$
\boxed{
u_{i,j}^{m+1}
=2u_{i,j}^m-u_{i,j}^{m-1}
+C_x^2(u_{i+1,j}^m-2u_{i,j}^m+u_{i-1,j}^m)
+C_y^2(u_{i,j+1}^m-2u_{i,j}^m+u_{i,j-1}^m)}
$$

where

$$
C_x=\frac{c\Delta t}{\Delta x},
\qquad
C_y=\frac{c\Delta t}{\Delta y}
$$

The stability condition is

$$
\boxed{C_x^2+C_y^2\le1}
$$

For equal spacing, $\Delta x=\Delta y=h$

$$
\Delta t\le\frac{h}{c\sqrt2}
$$

```python
next_values[1:-1, 1:-1] = (
    2 * current[1:-1, 1:-1]
    - previous[1:-1, 1:-1]
    + cx**2 * (current[2:, 1:-1] - 2 * current[1:-1, 1:-1] + current[:-2, 1:-1])
    + cy**2 * (current[1:-1, 2:] - 2 * current[1:-1, 1:-1] + current[1:-1, :-2])
)
previous, current, next_values = current, next_values, previous
```

## Nonlinear Convection

The inviscid Burgers equation is

$$
\boxed{u_t+uu_x=0}
$$

The wave speed is the solution itself, so different parts of a profile travel
at different speeds

The conservative form is

$$
\boxed{u_t+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
\left(\frac{u^2}{2}\right)=0}
$$

with flux

$$
F(u)=\frac{u^2}{2}
$$

### Conservative Discretisation

A conservative finite-volume update has the form

$$
u_i^{m+1}
=u_i^m-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}
\left(F_{i+1/2}^m-F_{i-1/2}^m\right)
$$

Flux leaving one control volume enters the next, so the discrete conserved
quantity changes only through boundary fluxes

Conservative schemes are essential when shocks appear because the differential
equation is not valid at the discontinuity, while the integral conservation
law remains meaningful

### Shock Formation

Along a characteristic of inviscid Burgers equation

$$
\frac{dx}{dt}=u
$$

and $u$ remains constant

Larger values can catch slower values, causing characteristics to intersect and
the gradient to become infinite

The resulting discontinuity travels according to the Rankine-Hugoniot speed

$$
\boxed{s=
\frac{F(u_R)-F(u_L)}{u_R-u_L}}
$$

For Burgers equation

$$
s=\frac{u_L+u_R}{2}
$$

## Exam Method

1. identify whether the PDE is first-order convection or a second-order wave equation
2. determine the direction and speed of information travel
3. calculate the appropriate CFL number
4. select the upwind side for first-order convection
5. preserve all required time levels for a wave equation
6. apply only inflow boundary data to pure one-way convection
7. check that a conservative form is used when shocks matter

> [!TIP] Common exam traps
>
> - using a left upwind stencil when $v<0$
> - comparing $C$ with $1/2$, which is the one-dimensional diffusion limit
> - forgetting the $-u_i^{m-1}$ term in the wave update
> - using the one-dimensional CFL limit for a two-dimensional wave problem
> - updating a three-level scheme without rotating the arrays correctly
> - discretising $uu_x$ non-conservatively across a shock
