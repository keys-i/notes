# Week 8 — Satellite Orbit

## Sun-Earth force balance

Let $r$ be the satellite's distance from the Sun and $R_E-r$ its distance from
Earth. For a circular orbit,

$$
\frac{GM_s}{r^2}
=\frac{GM_e}{(R_E-r)^2}+r\omega^2
$$

where

$$
G=6.67\times10^{-11},\quad
M_s=1.98\times10^{30},\quad
M_e=5.98\times10^{24}
$$

$$
R_E=1.49\times10^{11}\ \mathrm m,\qquad
T=3.15576\times10^7\ \mathrm s,\qquad
\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}
$$

Cancel the satellite mass and define

$$
f(r)=\frac{GM_s}{r^2}
-\frac{GM_e}{(R_E-r)^2}-r\omega^2
$$

The required orbit distance from Earth is $R_E-r$

## Newton iteration

$$
f'(r)=-\frac{2GM_s}{r^3}
-\frac{2GM_e}{(R_E-r)^3}-\omega^2
$$

$$
r_{k+1}=r_k-\frac{f(r_k)}{f'(r_k)}
$$

```python
def balance(radius):
    return (
        G * sun_mass / radius**2
        - (G * earth_mass / (earth_orbit - radius) ** 2)
        - radius * omega**2
    )
```

## Bracket first

Plot or sample $f(r)$ to find a sign-changing interval inside
$0<r<R_E$, then use bisection or a safeguarded Newton iteration

## Checks

- keep every distance in metres and $\omega$ in radians per second
- never evaluate at $r=0$ or $r=R_E$
- require both a small step and a small residual
- verify the initial interval has a sign change
- report $R_E-r$, not $r$, as the distance from Earth
