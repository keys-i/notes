# Week 10 - PDE Foundations and Parabolic Equations

## Partial Differential Equations

A partial differential equation contains derivatives with respect to more than
one independent variable

$$
u=u(x,t),
\qquad
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}
=F\left(u,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x},
\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2},\ldots\right)
$$

The highest derivative determines the order of the PDE

A PDE is linear when the field variable and all its derivatives appear only to
the first power and are not multiplied together

| Property       | Meaning                     |
| -------------- | --------------------------- |
| homogeneous    | no source or forcing term   |
| nonhomogeneous | a source term is present    |
| linear         | superposition applies       |
| nonlinear      | waves or modes can interact |

## PDE Classification

| Class      | Canonical example  | Physical behaviour                        | Data required                                     |
| ---------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| parabolic  | $u_t=D u_{xx}$     | diffusion and smoothing forward in time   | initial condition and spatial boundary conditions |
| hyperbolic | $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$ | disturbances travel at finite speed       | initial conditions and suitable boundaries        |
| elliptic   | $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$  | steady field influenced by every boundary | boundary conditions around the domain             |

The PDE class determines which numerical scheme and boundary information are
physically appropriate

> [!EXAM] Classify by information travel
>
> - parabolic equations smooth spatial disturbances and propagate information
>   with unlimited mathematical speed into the future, but not into the past
> - hyperbolic equations carry information at a finite speed; jumps can
>   propagate and nonlinear waves can form shocks
> - elliptic equations describe steady boundary-value problems whose boundary
>   data affect the whole domain, so they do not use an initial condition

## Grid Notation

Discretise space and time as

$$
x_i=x_0+i\Delta x,
\qquad
t^m=t_0+m\Delta t
$$

and write

$$
u_i^m\approx u(x_i,t^m)
$$

Useful finite differences are

$$
u_t(x_i,t^m)
\approx
\frac{u_i^{m+1}-u_i^m}{\Delta t}
$$

$$
u_{xx}(x_i,t^m)
\approx
\frac{u_{i+1}^m-2u_i^m+u_{i-1}^m}{\Delta x^2}
$$

## The Diffusion Equation

The one-dimensional diffusion equation is

$$
\boxed{\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}
=D\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}}
$$

where $D$ has units of length$^2$/time

For heat conduction

$$
D=\frac{k}{\rho C_p}
$$

where $k$ is thermal conductivity, $\rho$ is density and $C_p$ is specific heat

The equation combines conservation of energy with Fourier's law

$$
q=-kA\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}
$$

## Boundary and Initial Conditions

For a finite one-dimensional domain, a diffusion problem needs

- an initial field $u(x,0)$
- one condition at the left boundary
- one condition at the right boundary

### Dirichlet Boundary

The field value is prescribed

$$
u(0,t)=u_L(t)
$$

Set the boundary node directly at every time level

$$
u_0^{m+1}=u_L(t^{m+1})
$$

### Neumann Boundary

The normal gradient is prescribed

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}(0,t)=g(t)
$$

A first-order one-sided approximation gives

$$
\frac{u_1^{m+1}-u_0^{m+1}}{\Delta x}=g(t^{m+1})
$$

For an insulated boundary, $g=0$ and therefore

$$
u_0^{m+1}=u_1^{m+1}
$$

For heat conduction, convert an applied heat flux to a Neumann condition with
Fourier's law

$$
q_x=-k\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}
\approx-k\frac{T_1^{m+1}-T_0^{m+1}}{\Delta x}
$$

If $q_x$ is positive in the $+x$ direction, the left boundary value is

$$
\boxed{T_0^{m+1}=T_1^{m+1}+\frac{\Delta x\,q_x}{k}}
$$

If the stated flux $q_{\mathrm{out}}$ is instead positive outward from the left
boundary, then $q_{\mathrm{out}}=-q_x$ and the sign changes

$$
T_0^{m+1}=T_1^{m+1}-\frac{\Delta x\,q_{\mathrm{out}}}{k}
$$

> [!WARNING] Write the flux direction before choosing the sign
>
> The safe route is always $q=-kT_x$, substitute the one-sided difference,
> then solve for the boundary node

## Explicit Forward-Time Scheme

Substitute the forward time difference and central space difference into the
diffusion equation

$$
\frac{u_i^{m+1}-u_i^m}{\Delta t}
=
D\frac{u_{i+1}^m-2u_i^m+u_{i-1}^m}{\Delta x^2}
$$

Define the diffusion number

$$
\boxed{\sigma=\frac{D\Delta t}{\Delta x^2}}
$$

The explicit update is

$$
\boxed{u_i^{m+1}
=\sigma u_{i-1}^m+(1-2\sigma)u_i^m+\sigma u_{i+1}^m}
$$

It is first-order accurate in time and second-order accurate in space

$$
O(\Delta t)+O(\Delta x^2)
$$

```python
for step in range(time_steps):
    old = values.copy()
    values[1:-1] = sigma * old[:-2] + (1 - 2 * sigma) * old[1:-1] + sigma * old[2:]
    values[0] = left_boundary
    values[-1] = right_boundary
```

> [!WARNING] Calculate every new value from the old time level
>
> Updating the array in place from left to right silently changes the explicit scheme

### Explicit Matrix Form

For five nodes with fixed Dirichlet values at nodes $0$ and $4$, the lecture
writes the update as

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
u_0^{m+1}\\
u_1^{m+1}\\
u_2^{m+1}\\
u_3^{m+1}\\
u_4^{m+1}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\underbrace{
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0&0&0&0\\
\sigma&1-2\sigma&\sigma&0&0\\
0&\sigma&1-2\sigma&\sigma&0\\
0&0&\sigma&1-2\sigma&\sigma\\
0&0&0&0&1
\end{bmatrix}}_{S_D}
\begin{bmatrix}
u_0^m\\
u_1^m\\
u_2^m\\
u_3^m\\
u_4^m
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The first and last rows are boundary equations rather than diffusion stencils

For a zero-gradient boundary, $u_0^{m+1}=u_1^{m+1}$, so replace the first row
of $S_D$ by the row that updates node $1$

$$
(S_N)_{0,:}
=
\begin{bmatrix}\sigma&1-2\sigma&\sigma&0&0\end{bmatrix}
$$

The same idea at the right boundary copies the node-$3$ update into the last
row. A nonzero left flux adds $\Delta x\,q_x/k$ to the first entry of the
updated vector

## Stability of the Explicit Scheme

Von Neumann analysis substitutes a Fourier error mode into the update and
obtains the amplification factor

$$
G=1-2\sigma(1-\cos(k\Delta x))
$$

Stability requires

$$
|G|\le1
$$

for every wave number $k$, giving

$$
\boxed{0\le\sigma\le\frac12}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\Delta t\le\frac{\Delta x^2}{2D}}
$$

The smallest wavelengths are the most restrictive and begin to oscillate when
the limit is violated

> [!EXAMPLE]- Maximum stable time step
>
> Given $D=1.2\times10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$ and
> $\Delta x=2\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}$
>
> $$
> \Delta t_{\max}
> =\frac{(2\times10^{-3})^2}{2(1.2\times10^{-5})}
> =0.1667\ \text{s}
> $$
>
> A smaller time step may still be needed for accuracy

## Implicit Backward-Time Scheme

Evaluate the spatial derivative at the new time level

$$
\frac{u_i^{m+1}-u_i^m}{\Delta t}
=
D\frac{u_{i+1}^{m+1}-2u_i^{m+1}+u_{i-1}^{m+1}}
{\Delta x^2}
$$

Rearranging gives

$$
\boxed{-\sigma u_{i-1}^{m+1}
+(1+2\sigma)u_i^{m+1}
-\sigma u_{i+1}^{m+1}=u_i^m}
$$

### Implicit Matrix Form

For the same five-node Dirichlet problem

$$
\underbrace{
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0&0&0&0\\
-\sigma&1+2\sigma&-\sigma&0&0\\
0&-\sigma&1+2\sigma&-\sigma&0\\
0&0&-\sigma&1+2\sigma&-\sigma\\
0&0&0&0&1
\end{bmatrix}}_{H_D}
\begin{bmatrix}
u_0^{m+1}\\
u_1^{m+1}\\
u_2^{m+1}\\
u_3^{m+1}\\
u_4^{m+1}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
u_0^m\\
u_1^m\\
u_2^m\\
u_3^m\\
u_4^m
\end{bmatrix}
$$

For a time-varying Dirichlet value, replace the corresponding right-hand-side
entry by the prescribed value at $t^{m+1}$

For a left Neumann condition, replace the first matrix row and right-hand-side
entry with the boundary equation itself

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{condition} & (H_N)_{0,:} & b_0 \\
\hline
T_x=g & \begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0&0&0\end{bmatrix} & -g\Delta x \\
q_x=-kT_x & \begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0&0&0\end{bmatrix}
& \dfrac{q_x\Delta x}{k}
\end{array}
$$

An insulated boundary is the special case $b_0=0$

All new values are coupled, so each time step requires a tridiagonal linear solve

The method is unconditionally stable but still only first-order accurate in time

| Explicit                                  | Implicit                                |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| direct update                             | linear solve at every time step         |
| conditionally stable                      | unconditionally stable                  |
| cheap per step                            | more work per step                      |
| stability may force very small $\Delta t$ | accuracy still limits useful $\Delta t$ |

Unconditionally stable does not mean unconditionally accurate

## Exam Method

For a parabolic finite-difference problem

1. identify $D$, $\Delta x$ and $\Delta t$
2. calculate $\sigma=D\Delta t/\Delta x^2$
3. check $\sigma\le1/2$ before stepping an explicit scheme
4. write the stencil with correct time superscripts
5. apply initial values and boundary conditions separately
6. retain full precision during updates
7. interpret whether the result should diffuse and smooth

> [!TIP] Common exam traps
>
> - confusing the diffusion stability number with the hyperbolic CFL number
> - omitting the square on $\Delta x$
> - using a boundary equation as though it were an interior update
> - saying an implicit scheme is exact because it is unconditionally stable
> - overwriting old values during an explicit time step
