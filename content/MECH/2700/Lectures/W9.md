# Week 9 - Data Fitting and Optimisation

## Data Fitting

Data fitting replaces a large or noisy data set with a simpler model

Use fitting rather than interpolation when

- measurements contain noise
- there are many observations but only a few model parameters are wanted
- the model should describe the overall trend rather than pass through every point

For data $(x_i,y_i)$ and a model $p(x)$, the residual at point $i$ is

$$
r_i=y_i-p(x_i)
$$

Least squares chooses the model parameters that minimise the sum of squared errors

$$
S=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}r_i^2
=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}[y_i-p(x_i)]^2
$$

Squaring prevents positive and negative residuals cancelling and penalises
large errors strongly

| Interpolation                                            | Least-squares fitting                            |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| passes through every data point                          | approximates the overall data trend              |
| normally uses as many conditions as unknown coefficients | normally has more observations than coefficients |
| appropriate for accurate tabulated values                | appropriate for noisy or over-sampled values     |
| high order may oscillate                                 | a deliberately low-order model can be robust     |

> [!WARNING] A smaller training error does not always mean a better model
>
> A high-order polynomial can fit noise and behave badly between or beyond the data

## Polynomial Least Squares

Choose a polynomial with $m$ coefficients and degree at most $m-1$

$$
p(x)=\alpha_0+\alpha_1x+\cdots+\alpha_{m-1}x^{m-1}
=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\alpha_kx^k
$$

Usually $m<N$, where $N$ is the number of observations

At the least-squares minimum

$$
\frac{\partial S}{\partial\alpha_j}=0,
\qquad j=0,1,\ldots,m-1
$$

Differentiating and rearranging gives the normal equations

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\alpha_k
\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}x_i^{j+k}\right)
=
\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}y_ix_i^j
$$

### Design Matrix Form

Construct the $N\times m$ design matrix

$$
X=
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & x_0 & x_0^2 & \cdots & x_0^{m-1}\\
1 & x_1 & x_1^2 & \cdots & x_1^{m-1}\\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots\\
1 & x_{N-1} & x_{N-1}^2 & \cdots & x_{N-1}^{m-1}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then

$$
X\boldsymbol{\alpha}\approx\mathbf y
$$

and the normal equations are

$$
\boxed{X^TX\boldsymbol{\alpha}=X^T\mathbf y}
$$

The residual vector is orthogonal to every column of $X$

$$
X^T(\mathbf y-X\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\mathbf0
$$

> [!NOTE] Numerical implementation
>
> The normal equations are useful for hand calculations and exam derivations,
> but QR or SVD is usually more stable in software because forming $X^TX$
> squares the condition number

### Straight-Line Regression

For

$$
p(x)=\alpha_0+\alpha_1x
$$

the normal equations reduce to

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
N & \sum x_i\\
\sum x_i & \sum x_i^2
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\alpha_0\\
\alpha_1
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\sum y_i\\
\sum x_iy_i
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Equivalently

$$
\alpha_1=
\frac{\overline{xy}-\bar x\bar y}
{\overline{x^2}-\bar x^2},
\qquad
\alpha_0=\bar y-\alpha_1\bar x
$$

> [!EXAMPLE]- Fit a line to three observations
>
> Fit $y=\alpha_0+\alpha_1x$ to $(0,1)$, $(1,2)$ and $(2,2)$
>
> $$
> N=3,\quad \sum x_i=3,\quad \sum x_i^2=5,
> \quad \sum y_i=5,\quad \sum x_iy_i=6
> $$
>
> $$
> \begin{bmatrix}3&3\\3&5\end{bmatrix}
> \begin{bmatrix}\alpha_0\\\alpha_1\end{bmatrix}
> =
> \begin{bmatrix}5\\6\end{bmatrix}
> $$
>
> Solving gives
>
> $$
> \alpha_0=\frac76,
> \qquad
> \alpha_1=\frac12
> $$
>
> so $p(x)=7/6+x/2$

## General Basis Functions

A fitted model need not use monomials

$$
p(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\alpha_j\phi_j(x)
$$

Construct

$$
\Phi_{ij}=\phi_j(x_i)
$$

and solve

$$
\boxed{\Phi^T\Phi\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\Phi^T\mathbf y}
$$

Possible basis functions include

- $1,x,x^2,\ldots$ for polynomial models
- $1,e^x,e^{-x}$ for exponential behaviour
- $1,\sin x,\cos x$ for periodic behaviour
- physically motivated functions from an engineering model

The method remains linear least squares provided the unknown coefficients enter
linearly, even if the basis functions are nonlinear in $x$

## Chebyshev Polynomial Basis

The first Chebyshev polynomials are

$$
T_0(z)=1,
\qquad
T_1(z)=z
$$

with recurrence

$$
\boxed{T_{n+1}(z)=2zT_n(z)-T_{n-1}(z)}
$$

For example

$$
T_2(z)=2z^2-1,
\qquad
T_3(z)=4z^3-3z
$$

Chebyshev functions are a better-conditioned basis than high powers of $x$
because their shapes remain distinct over $[-1,1]$

Scale data from $[a,b]$ to $[-1,1]$ using

$$
\boxed{z_i=\frac{2x_i-a-b}{b-a}}
$$

Then fit

$$
p(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\alpha_jT_j(z(x))
$$

## Assessing a Fit

Always inspect more than the coefficient values

1. plot the data and fitted curve
2. inspect residuals $r_i$
3. calculate the sum of squared errors $S$
4. check whether the model has physically sensible units and shape
5. avoid extrapolation unless the model has physical justification

Increasing polynomial degree cannot increase the least-squares error on the
same data, but it can worsen conditioning and predictive quality

## Optimisation

An optimisation problem chooses design variables $\mathbf x$ to minimise an
objective function

$$
\boxed{\min_{\mathbf x}f(\mathbf x)}
$$

To maximise $g$, minimise $-g$

The objective may combine competing engineering goals, such as mass, drag,
cost and stiffness, but the scaling and weighting must be physically justified

### Local and Global Minima

A local minimum $x^*$ satisfies

$$
f(x^*)\le f(x)
$$

for points $x$ in a neighbourhood of $x^*$

A global minimum satisfies this over the entire feasible domain

One-dimensional methods can guarantee the minimum only under assumptions about
the interval, such as unimodality

### Unconstrained and Constrained Problems

| Type          | Meaning                                                                   |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| unconstrained | the design variables may take any value in the search domain              |
| constrained   | equalities, inequalities or physical bounds restrict the feasible designs |

MECH2700 focuses on one-dimensional unconstrained minimisation

## Bracketing a Minimum

Three ordered points $a<b<c$ bracket a local minimum when

$$
\boxed{f(a)>f(b)\le f(c)}
$$

A practical bracketing search moves downhill and expands the step until the
function rises again

Bracketing is important because golden-section search never leaves its starting
interval

## Golden-Section Search

Golden-section search reduces a bracket containing one minimum without derivatives

Let

$$
g=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\approx0.618034
$$

Choose

$$
x_L=a+(1-g)(b-a),
\qquad
x_R=a+g(b-a)
$$

For minimisation

- if $f(x_L)<f(x_R)$, keep $[a,x_R]$
- otherwise, keep $[x_L,b]$

The geometry lets one interior point and its function value be reused, so only
one new function evaluation is needed per iteration

```python
from math import sqrt


def golden_section(f, a, b, tolerance):
    ratio = (sqrt(5) - 1) / 2
    left = a + (1 - ratio) * (b - a)
    right = a + ratio * (b - a)
    f_left = f(left)
    f_right = f(right)

    while b - a > tolerance:
        if f_left < f_right:
            b = right
            right = left
            f_right = f_left
            left = a + (1 - ratio) * (b - a)
            f_left = f(left)
        else:
            a = left
            left = right
            f_left = f_right
            right = a + ratio * (b - a)
            f_right = f(right)

    return (a + b) / 2
```

After $n$ reductions

$$
L_n=g^nL_0
$$

To guarantee $L_n\le\epsilon$

$$
n\ge
\left\lceil
\frac{\ln(\epsilon/L_0)}{\ln g}
\right\rceil
$$

> [!WARNING] Golden-section search requires unimodality
>
> The objective must be unimodal on the starting interval
>
> With several local minima, it may converge to one local minimum and miss the
> global solution

## Exam Method

### Least-Squares Question

1. state the proposed model and unknown coefficients
2. build the design matrix or write the normal equations
3. solve for the coefficients
4. evaluate the model in the requested direction
5. report units and, if requested, residual error

### Golden-Section Question

1. confirm the task is minimisation and the interval is suitable
2. calculate $x_L$ and $x_R$
3. evaluate and compare $f(x_L)$ and $f(x_R)$
4. discard the impossible side without reversing the inequality
5. reuse one old point and evaluate only one new point
6. stop using the stated interval or point tolerance

> [!TIP] Common exam traps
>
> - fitting $x$ as a function of $y$ when the requested calibration is $y(x)$
> - using degree $m$ when $m$ denotes the number of coefficients
> - confusing a root bracket $f(a)f(b)<0$ with a minimum bracket $f(a)>f(b)<f(c)$
> - claiming golden-section search finds a global minimum without unimodality
